What DNO application support actually covers

Most installers think of the DNO application as one form. It isn't. It's a sequence, and each stage is somewhere a job can stall.

Form selection

Getting this wrong is the most common reason applications bounce. A 4kW array on single phase and a 4kW array on three phase go down completely different routes. So does the same system with a battery attached, or with an export limiter fitted.

Technical data

Inverter make and model, type test certificate reference, G100 export limitation scheme details where relevant, MPAN, phase configuration, existing generation on site. Every network operator wants this in a slightly different format and rejects it in a slightly different way.

Submission

Six network operator groups, six portals, six sets of quirks. Some accept email. Some require an account with a per-user login. Some time out and lose your entry if you take too long over the technical section.

Query handling

This is where most installer time actually goes. The DNO comes back asking for a single-line diagram, or a clarification on the export limit, or a corrected type test reference. If nobody answers within their window, the application drops out of the queue and you start again.

Approval and export MPAN

The approval and the export MPAN are separate things and don't always arrive together. Your customer can't register for SEG without the export MPAN, so someone has to chase it.

What you send us

Deliberately short:

  • Install address and postcode
  • MPAN (import)
  • System size in kWp and inverter make/model
  • Single or three phase
  • Battery storage, yes or no, and capacity
  • Export limited, yes or no, and the limit
  • Customer name and contact details

That's it. If something's missing or looks off, we come back to you before submission rather than after rejection.

Timescales

Our desk and the network operator's queue are two different clocks.

G98Notifications

Submitted within one working day of receiving your details. These are notify-after-install, so the constraint is the 28 day notification window, not the DNO's response time.

G99Applications

Submitted within one to two working days. The network operator then has its own statutory window to respond, typically up to 45 working days for a standard distributed generation application, though many come back faster and fast-track routes exist for smaller Type A systems.

Our average end-to-end turnaround is seven days

Across all application types. Where a job runs longer, it's the DNO's queue, not our desk — and you'll know where it is, because we tell you.

Can you not just do this yourself?

Yes. Nothing about a DNO application requires a licence or a qualification. If you want to run them in-house, you can.

Here's the honest arithmetic on why most installers stop.

30–40 min

A straightforward G98 notification, for an experienced person who knows the portal

2–4 hrs

A G99 with a battery and export limiter, the first few times. Closer to 90 minutes once fluent

2–4 hrs

Realistic all-in per job, once you add query handling and export MPAN chasing

Then there's the failure cost. Applications get rejected for reasons that have nothing to do with the quality of the install — a superseded type test reference, the wrong form for the phase configuration, a missing single-line diagram, an export limitation scheme described in language the assessor doesn't accept. A rejection doesn't just cost the resubmission time, it puts you back to the end of a queue that may be weeks long, with a customer already asking when their system goes live.

The maths is usually simple. If your charge-out rate is anything above £30 an hour, doing it yourself costs more than sending it to us.

The other argument for keeping it in-house is control, and that one's fair. Which is why we don't take the job off you — we send you the submission confirmation, the reference, every DNO exchange and the approval. You keep the audit trail. You just don't spend the afternoon on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does DNO application support cost?

We work on a flat fee per application rather than an hourly rate, so you know the cost before you commit. G98 notifications and G99 applications are priced separately because the work involved is materially different. Full pricing is on our pricing page.

Do I need to give you portal access?

No. We submit under our own credentials with your details, or under yours if you prefer to keep submissions in your own account. Both work. Some installers prefer the second option for audit purposes.

What happens if the DNO rejects the application?

We handle it. Correcting and resubmitting a rejected application is part of the service, not an extra. If the rejection is because information you supplied was wrong, we'll tell you what needs changing and turn the resubmission around the same day.

Can you get the export MPAN as well as the approval?

Yes, and we chase it separately if it doesn't arrive with the approval. Your customer needs the export MPAN to register for the Smart Export Guarantee, so it's the thing that actually finishes the job.

Do you handle applications with battery storage?

Yes. Battery systems change the application route and the technical data required, particularly around export limitation and the combined inverter rating. It's one of the more common places DIY applications go wrong.

Do you cover commercial as well as domestic?

Yes. Domestic work is mostly G98 and smaller G99. Commercial rooftop runs through G99, including larger Type A and Type B systems where the technical requirements step up considerably.

How quickly can you submit?

One to two working days from receiving complete job details, usually same day for G98.
